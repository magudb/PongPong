Painted seasonal festivals — continuation of Claude's I5 art handoff

Scope: art/design on codex-review. The unfinished m1b gameplay is not merged or
edited. This is the I5 art and placement-design delivery, not completion of its
TownDirector / SocialManager runtime package. Those integration gates remain
explicitly pending in the handoff and tests.

The kit contains the exact 24 published IDs and 53 authored views: two bunting
strings, four seasonal banner poles, two lantern strings, a shared stage and
bench, maypole, bonfire, pumpkin pile, wheat sheaves, flower arch, ice lantern,
four seasonal market stalls and four door wreaths. Four-view stage and stalls
have independently painted fronts and closed backs. No view is a mirror flag
or a reused front presented as another authored quarter-turn. No lettering,
faces or effigies are baked into the decoration.

Style follows the accepted characters, city and tiles: matte storybook gouache
and watercolor with warm irregular pencil edges, honey timber, cream cloth,
sage, muted lavender, terracotta, small sky-blue and dull brass accents. Spring
uses blossoms, summer honey/sun details, autumn wheat/leaves/pumpkins, and winter
evergreen, a pale snowflake and ice candles. The old soft-CG template direction
is superseded by the chosen painted style, as in the core/specialist shop kits.

Built-in imagegen generated five full-size source sheets and the revised tall
pole sheet. The initial short poles could not support overhead strings at the
character scale; poles.png preserves the approved seasonal identities on longer
shafts. The exact edit prompt and its retained seasonal.png input are recorded
alongside all five generation jobs in prompts.json. Six selected master files
are under tools/art/drawn/sources/festival/. sources.json retains their hashes
and dimensions. Repacking the selected paintings needs no credentials or online
generation. asset-repair-prompt.txt is the reusable future correction template.

The first native-size export exceeded the 7 MB all-view upload estimate. The
export now uses 82% texture resolution and an explicit reciprocal render_scale,
preserving the same world size, ground anchors and string span. Full-resolution
masters remain saved. Body and child light-mask transforms are applied once.
The original footprints, layers and authored-view counts are unchanged. This
is export-resolution control, not a smaller festival in the game world.

Strings use a first-post ground anchor and span three cells: (+192,+114) or
(-192,+114) world pixels. Rope attachments sit 225 px above their ground posts.
Wreaths use the first wall/door-cell ground anchor and a 95 px mount height.
Floor props sort at their ground centre; overhead strings use a foreground
plane. All decoration is non-solid and adds zero PointLight2D nodes. Six cropped
additive night masks cover the two orientations of both lantern strings, the
bonfire and ice lantern. They follow the painted light sources, without a new
radial light or an opaque glow rectangle.

festival.json exposes seven named slots for each season: pole, string, stage,
bench, centre, awning and door; spring flower arch and autumn sheaves are extra
season accents. lua/config/festival_decor.lua is literal declarative art data:
nine fixed festival IDs plus the date-dependent wedding, seasonal set, centre
override, slots used, door-wreath choice and placement relative to standard post
suffixes. contracts.json records the calendar generator and 24-post set sources
read-only, without executing the content generators or installing social data.

PaintedFestivalArt is a stateless presentation helper. It accepts resolved
positions and a chosen event; it does not start festivals, read calendar state,
subscribe to game events, create an autoload or change saves. Four awnings reuse
one selected texture. Only the views actually used by an event are loaded;
manifest caching retains JSON data rather than texture objects. The art gate
checks resource release after its holder is freed, separately from GPU allocator
pool behavior. No live town director is attached by this art delivery.

Budgets: 0.7 MB disk, 7 MB all 53 views plus six masks as conservative RGBA8 with
complete mip chains, and 1.8 MB for an event's selected views/masks. The last
limit is NOT a promise that every authored rotation in a seasonal library is
resident within 1.8 MB: unused rotations must remain unloaded. packing.json and
event-budgets.json retain the separate totals. Shared town and character textures
are outside the festival-only budget. These estimates are not whole-game VRAM
or frame-time measurements. Saved rebuild timings are observations on this host.

The developer gallery previews four seasons by day/evening on installed native
Hyggebyen and Torvet geometry. The original standard Torvet post block spilled
past the front rim and placed the host in the existing fire pit. Its final art
study therefore uses hand-placed positions, two temporary stalls and four
guests on that platform. This is a proposed art arrangement; it does not prove
the unfinished director's post snapping, NPC routing or gameplay access. The
standard 24-post schema remains unchanged in the data contract. Contact sheets
include every authored view, even pieces not used by a particular town fixture.

The old validate_features_home.py named in the historical work order validates
the A1 furniture/home catalog and CG symmetry/counts, not festival.json. Current
art_festival tests cover this painted kit; the existing art_home and world style
gates run in regression checks. Lua data is linted with the project's real tool.

Final measurements, checks and inspected screenshot hashes: validation.json.
Review evidence: native/, headless-tests.json, native-tests.json, full-tests.json.
Runtime integration and remaining wardrobe production: handoff.txt.

Final export: 571,494 disk bytes, 6,636,896 conservative RGBA8/mip bytes for the
complete library, and at most 1,109,160 such bytes for a selected event. All 59
textures and the manifest rebuild byte-identically. The observed export used
1.41 user CPU seconds (2.09 wall seconds with profiling); the separate warm
byte-identical repack took 3.37 wall seconds. WebP quality is 90, method 4,
with lossless accents; source masters remain full-resolution.

Verification: 1,130 focused checks across festival, world style and specialist
shop sections, zero failures and four explicit pending-integration skips. The
festival section itself passes 299 checks with two live-integration skips.
Windowed: 57 checks, zero failures/skips, all 24 captures inspected. The full
strict animal regression passes 5,015 checks across 35 sections with 59 named
pending-owner skips. All runs have zero engine errors and warnings. The full
suite's 26 allowed messages belong to existing scoped negative/smoke cases.
The actual Lua lint passes all 74 files with zero findings.
