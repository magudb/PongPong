Painted core shop kit — continuation of Claude's art handoff

This pass stays on codex-review. Claude's unfinished m1b implementation is a
read-only source for the consumer contract. No gameplay branch is merged or edited.

The previous interior paintings supplied one front view of the service pieces.
The upcoming towns PieceCatalog instead reads assets/iso_props/shops/core/shops.json
and emblems.json. The new kit fills that exact interface: 20 piece IDs with 51
authored views, 15 separate 96 px profession badges, and five trimmed ember masks.
The 16 service-piece stand-ins, the counter/end/hatch and sign bracket are covered.

Style follows the accepted characters, tiles and interior paintings: matte gouache,
warm irregular pencil, honey timber, muted sage, cream, terracotta and charcoal
iron. The images were generated with built-in imagegen. Five full-size masters
are under tools/art/drawn/sources/shop_core/. Exact prompts and ordered reference
paths are in prompts.json. sources.json records their hashes. No live generation
or credentials are needed to repack them.

IDs, layers, footprints, fallback IDs and authored-view counts follow the I3
contract in shop_core_layout.json. The old soft-CG template instructions are
superseded by the accepted painted direction. Room layouts are unchanged.
The art-only copies of all 14 core room layouts in room-contracts.json came from
the existing towns generator. They do not install a competing room layout system.

Floor pivots remain on the ground plane. Wall pieces are projected onto the
isometric wall axes and mounted 45 px above the first wall cell's ground anchor,
matching the towns builder's placement convention. New core pieces sort at their
ground centre. The first native review
found that using the long counter's left-corner y caused the keeper to sort in
front and appear to stand on the worktop. Ground-centre sorting corrects it.
The native gate checks that feet are hidden and the head remains visible.
Sign-bracket origins align with the separate badge below the chain. No labels or
localized glyphs are painted into the art.

The prescribed authored-view counts are explicit: four for the counters, ovens,
forge, anvil, mannequin and tub; two for rectangular work pieces and wall pieces;
one for the small orientation-neutral piles/cauldron. Rear oven/forge views have
solid backs and no fire mask. Unsupported extra views are not presented as
authored art by the gallery's reader. No export uses a mirror flag.

packing.json records disk and conservative RGBA8+mip sizes. The existing limits
remain 0.8 MB on disk and 8.8 MB resident for this core kit, with a 150 KB mean
base-sprite allowance. Per-room kit estimates have a 3 MB cap; they exclude the
existing shared furniture, wall/floor and character atlases. These estimates are
not a whole-game VRAM measurement or a frame-time benchmark.
The installed art uses 719,856 bytes on disk and 8,777,480 conservative resident
bytes. Bodies are quality-90 WebP; badges and light masks are lossless. Imports
use S3TC with mipmaps. The packer itself refuses an export above either budget.

The native evidence includes every imported view, every badge on its bracket,
all 14 core rooms in daylight with their registered keeper visuals, and bakery,
smithy and bathhouse comparisons against the former stand-ins and by evening.
These are art review scenes built from the real layout snapshots,
painted tiles/walls, furniture and character resources, without gameplay managers.
Reports: full-tests.json, headless-tests.json and native-tests.json. Images: native/.
The focused headless run passes 627 checks, with the one explicit pending live
towns-table check; the native run passes 51 checks with no skips, and the equipment
studio passes 129 checks. All three have zero engine errors and warnings. The
native foot-occlusion check compares against a counter-hidden negative control;
it allows faint painted-edge/readback differences instead of demanding exact
zero pixel differences from a translucent painted surface.
The full strict animal regression suite passes 3,990 checks across 33 sections,
with zero failures, engine errors or engine warnings. Its 55 skips name their
pending owners; the 26 allowed messages belong to explicit existing test cases.

The equipment design and studio now display the blue ghost as Pedro, matching
the existing HERO_BU_NAME / SKIN_GHOST_BU translations. Internal bu/ghost_bu IDs
and the historical exact generation prompts retain their original names.
