Held equipment: production art and fit review

The selected tool or weapon is visible on all eight playable heroes. A persistent
parchment readout shows its inventory icon, translated name and Selected, Using or
Weapon context. Farm and town use the hotbar selection; action maps use the
equipped weapon. An action retains its actual item until completion, even if the
player changes selection or equipment. Empty and unavailable choices are named.
Dialogue, fainting and sleep put the object away.

Painting and provenance

The built-in image generation tool produced the dedicated held-object paintings,
using the approved equipment kit and sword turnaround as references. Exact prompts,
reference paths, selected source and rejected first sheet are in prompts.json.
The first sheet's two hoes read as axes; a targeted repair gave them rectangular
cultivating blades. The production atlas has 14 items in eight directional views:
two hoes, two watering cans, three pickaxes, an axe, scythe, rod, two swords,
a grooming brush and a milking pail. The last two use a separate 4x4 companion
painting (1254 by 1254), fitted into columns 13 and 14 of the 1792 by 1024
production atlas. Packing resamples each isolated care-tool cell to 128 pixels.
The original tool master is 1536 by 1024, with 128-pixel cells. The original generation
prompt requested a larger canvas; the selected source's actual size is recorded
here and used by the packer.

tools/art/drawn/held_equipment.py packs the selected source losslessly and records
the explicit view pivots in assets/held_equipment/manifest.json. The production
atlas is 7 MiB uncompressed RGBA with no mipmaps. There are no new body paintings
or inventory icon replacements. All 2,562 previously reviewed visual/theme assets
remain byte-identical. The newly reviewed atlas is the only added visual asset.

Visual review

The repaired master was inspected for silhouettes, materials, transparency and
separate directional objects. Warm pencil edges, matte timber, restrained copper,
brass and iron match docs/art/style-guide.txt and the equipment references.
The all-heroes-contact.png in docs/qa/held-equipment shows the actual native game
renderer on fox, monkey, both fairies, both hobbits and both ghosts. The native
captures also include each hero with the iron sword in the forest. The catalog
contact under native-animal shows all 14 items with their matching readout at
normal game zoom. The watering cans hang below their carry handles; the rod,
scythe and hoe keep distinct outer silhouettes. Existing character faces, wings,
tails and proportions remain readable. These are current stock-body carry poses,
not the unfinished clothing concepts from the equipment studio.

Runtime fit and ownership

The shipped body animation is the sole clock. The manifest has 3,056 explicit
body/clip/direction/frame grips across the eight heroes, derived offline from
individually authored stock-body contact points. The renderer performs no image
matching. Tool paintings and a narrow patch from the matching live hand texture
follow the current frame. Rear views draw behind the body; front views put the
matching hand over the handle. Both roots are children of the real body Sprite,
so altitude, scale, spin, tint and flashes inherit once. No global depth override
or second body layer is used. Reparenting the actor during travel retains them.

Player2D resolves the presentation from the existing hotbar, equipment and action
snapshot. Combat uses the same weapon that the action names. There is no second
inventory, save format, damage source, animation timer or collision shape. The
HUD subscribes to changes and language updates rather than polling every frame.
The existing HUD remains available in the forest while its separate HUD scene is
absent, preserving health, prompts and the weapon readout.

This delivery uses one near-hand carry contact and the existing work/attack poses.
Extended swings, authored support-hand/two-handed poses and fitted clothing remain
the separate packages described in docs/art/equipment/design.txt. The current
renderer does not claim those later poses or replace the approved body animation.

Validation and performance

See docs/qa/held-equipment/acceptance.json and README.txt for the retained reports,
native screenshots, source-bound town proofs and measured rendering costs.
The existing 250-draw city/HUD baseline remains enforced independently of the
equipment increment (budget: 16). Full frame time and total texture memory include
the held equipment and readout. No frame-time or texture-memory limit was raised.
