Painted interiors — homes, shops and home upgrades

Visual direction

Stardew Valley is the inspiration for cozy functional rooms, recognizable work
areas, welcoming everyday clutter and a home that grows with the player. Our
interpretation keeps Trætoppen's approved painted characters, buildings and tiles:
matte gouache, warm grey pencil edges, worn honey timber, sage cloth, cream plaster,
quiet lavender, terracotta and small brass details. Rooms retain the game's
128x76 isometric grid and elevated 36-degree camera. This is not a pixel-art pack.
Reference pointer: https://www.stardewvalley.net/media/

The cut-away back walls keep the whole room readable. Beds, storage and work
areas have space beside them; entrances stay clear. Public shop areas sit in
front of the counter and the keeper's work corridor sits behind it. The bathhouse
and fairy king's hall use open layouts rather than a counter across the room.
No words are painted onto signs: emblems communicate the profession and localized
labels remain the UI's responsibility. Window light comes from aligned masks.

Home progression

  1 Skur: patched pine bed, storage chest and small workbench; plain timber/stone.
  2 Hytte: first fitted kitchen, warm hearth, tea table, chair and houseplant.
  3 Straatag: a loom and bookcase join the cottage; timber floor, sprig plaster.
  4 Bindingsvaerk: oak double bed, wardrobe and soft sage sofa; more living space.
  5 Gaarden: separate-feeling cooking, craft and sitting areas; paper/parquet.
  6 Taarnhus: writing desk, comfortable reading chair and taller library bookcase.
  7 Landsted: piano, long dining table and an inviting carved stone fireplace.
  8 Herregaard: canopy bed, richer chairs, library and plants; warm marble shell.
  9 Borg: leaf-crystal throne joins the established home furnishings.
 10 Slot: chandelier, library, music, dining and craft furnishings share the larger
    authored footprint. The palace still contains familiar useful furniture.

Every tier study reads the existing room_tNN.json floor, doorway and capacity.
The furnishings are composition examples, not new upgrade rewards or free items.
Material choices can be selected independently of footprint growth. Costs, gates,
ownership, upgrade timing and player-arranged furniture remain gameplay data.

Town rooms

The 24 room IDs and dimensions follow TOWNS_FARM_SOCIAL_ADDENDUM sections 4.1/4.4:
the nine Hyggebyen shops, Brumme's workshop, the post office, owl appraisal room,
bathhouse, town hall, inn, fairy king's hall and eight citizens' homes. Each has a
furnishing study. Professions have their own main silhouettes: bread oven/racks,
forge/anvil, tailor's mannequin/loom, postal pigeonholes/parcels, seed plants/sacks,
timber workbench/planks, furniture displays, and a magic cauldron/library.
Citizens' homes reuse the common kit with occupation-specific accents.

Deliverables

Six imagegen masters: tools/art/drawn/sources/interior_{domestic,shops,regal,
walls,emblems,dressing}.png. Generated with the built-in imagegen tool; the exact prompts,
reference roles and saved master paths are in prompts.json beside this document.

103 native exports in assets/iso_props/painted_interiors/:
  52 furnishing/service/dressing sprites, including the 20 functional shop-kit
     pieces, three floor rugs, lamps and seed/potion/fabric displays;
  15 profession/home emblem plates;
  28 wall/corner pieces for timber, sprig plaster, ivory paper and warm marble;
   8 additive window masks, derived from those painted window panes.
The existing painted floor atlases supply stone, plank, parquet and marble.
The original interior manifest now resolves its 21 wall/corner pieces to this
painted pack, with the existing sizes/pivots and unchanged door-piece entries.

assets/data/interiors/painted.json contains the 34 furnishing studies, floor
footprints, placed pieces, clear public floor and staff bands. It is generated,
not an alternative source of gameplay room dimensions. Home geometry comes from
the existing tier room JSON files; town dimensions come from the towns addendum.
scripts/art/painted_interior_art.gd provides read access and footprint positioning.

Scope

This delivers the art kit, exported assets and inspectable native room studies.
The current M1a checkout has no live home/town interior builder or upgrade service.
The gallery does not install doors, opening hours, shops, ownership or a second
upgrade system. Those existing planned gameplay packages can consume this kit.
Sprites claim their one authored front-right view. They do not claim to replace
all 130 existing furniture items or supply their four rotation views. The original
furniture collection remains intact. Service-specific graphics are ready as art;
this is not a claim that the I3 gameplay/integration work package is complete.

Rebuild and inspect

  tools/art/.venv/bin/python tools/art/drawn/pack.py --only=interiors
  tests/run_playtest.sh --strict --no-boot --only=art_interiors,art_home,art_world_style,art_environment

Interactive native art gallery (left/right to browse, N for day/evening):
  tools/dev/play.sh --throwaway tools/art/drawn/interiors_gallery.tscn

Reproduce the native captures using the project's window lock and throwaway profile:
  GODOT_EXTRA_ARGS=--minimized tools/dev/run_windowed.sh --strict --no-boot \
    --sections-dir=res://tests/art_review --only=interior_views --out=/tmp/interior-views

The renderer is tools/art/drawn/interiors_view.gd. It uses the real tile atlases,
painted prop resources, authored geometry and character animation sprites.
The scene is an art gallery, not a gameplay screenshot. The headless checks cover
resource selection, home geometry, overlapping footprints, clear entrances and
connected public floor. Room-specific texture estimates exclude shared character
atlases; they are not whole-game VRAM or frame-time measurements.

Saved checks and measured limits are in validation.txt. The previews directory
contains all 34 daylight studies and five evening variants captured in Godot.
