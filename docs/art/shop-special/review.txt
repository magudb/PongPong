Painted specialist shops — continuation of Claude's I4 art handoff

This work stays on codex-review and covers art/design only. Claude's unfinished
m1b implementation is a read-only source for the piece and room contracts. No
gameplay branch is merged, installed or edited.

The I4 delivery adds 69 piece IDs with 115 independently authored views across
15 specialist sets: general shop storage and small accessories, seed shop, post
office, bathhouse, town hall, Hoppe's potting room, bakery, smithy, magic/owls,
animal shop, timber shop, furniture shop, tailor, workshop and flea market.
The exact footprint, layer and authored-view counts follow the published art
contract in tools/art/drawn/shop_special_layout.json. The previously accepted
matte gouache and warm pencil direction supersedes the old soft-CG instructions.

The paintings use the existing core workroom/accessory masters and fox portrait
as references: honey timber, sage, cream, lavender, terracotta and dull iron or
brass; irregular warm outlines, visible brush texture and friendly readable
shapes. Built-in imagegen generated the source sheets. Nine selected full-size
masters live in tools/art/drawn/sources/shop_special/. Exact prompts, reference
paths and any repair instructions are retained in prompts.json. sources.json
records the selected master hashes. No API key or online generation is needed
to rebuild the selected art.

The exporter extracts whole alpha islands, scales their paintings and supplies
native mounting metadata. It does not invent alternate views by flipping or
stretching the same picture into four rotations. Four-view mechanical/display
pieces include separately painted backs. Wall pieces follow the 64:38 wall
axis, anchored at the first wall cell. Their mounting heights vary by function:
small boards hang higher; the fitting curtain reaches down to the floor.
Tabletop pivots raise the picture 60 px above its ground anchor; ceiling props
use 112 px. A 19 px tabletop sort offset places accessories after their support.

Hanging stars and steam have separate cropped, lossless additive accent masks
derived from the new painting. The body remains readable in daylight. These
accents request zero point lights. The gallery keeps at most the one existing
core fire light, within the existing four-light room limit.

Optional dressing lives in data/interiors/dress/int_<room>.json. All 14 core
shop rooms and the generated Hoppe room have 2–6 removable pieces. Existing
room geometry is kept in its owner package; the 24 generator snapshots under
room-contracts.json are art inputs, not competing live room definitions.
The generator prefers the functional pieces before clutter, reserves customer
and keeper approaches, leaves doors and citizen posts reachable, avoids window
spans when hanging wall art, and requires tabletop accessories to fit completely
on a supporting surface. The 15 overlays contain 84 placements. dressing.json
records the pieces omitted for space or the full-room texture budget.

The actual towns validator has also been run read-only against every overlay.
towns-validator.json records its source hash and all input hashes so stale
evidence fails the art check. An independent flood-fill check in art_shops_b.gd
also verifies access and includes blocked-door/post negative controls.

Budgets are the existing I4 limits: 1.4 MB on disk and 15.4 MB conservative
RGBA8 including all mip levels. Two room checks retain the 3 MB cap: a
conservative RGBA8 core-plus-specialist kit estimate, and the actual imported
room texture estimate including shell, floor, existing furniture and accents.
Shared character atlases are separate. These are texture estimates, not a
whole-game VRAM or frame-time measurement. packing.json records exact export
totals and per-set allocations; the packer refuses an over-budget kit.
The selected export is 873,474 disk bytes and 10,869,824 RGBA8+mip bytes.

Verification and native review results are recorded in validation.json.
Native evidence includes every imported view in 15 set contact sheets, all 15
dressed rooms in daylight AND evening, and bare/dressed bakery, smithy and tailor
comparisons, including the steam and star accents. The gallery uses the actual painted
tiles, wall kit, furniture and registered character visuals without gameplay
managers or save writes. See native/, headless-tests.json, native-tests.json and
full-tests.json. Integration boundaries and remaining art work are in handoff.txt.
The focused headless gate passes 1,352 checks with three explicit pending
integration skips. The windowed gate passes 149 checks with no skips and
produces 48 inspected captures (15 set sheets and 33 room views/comparisons).
The full strict animal regression passes 4,715 checks across 34 sections with
57 named pending-owner skips. All runs have zero failures, engine errors and
engine warnings. The full suite's 26 allowed messages are scoped existing
negative/smoke cases, not unexpected errors.

The first full-room texture check found that the furniture showroom exceeded
3 MB. Its existing evening shell/furniture already cost 2.984 MB. This room now
keeps only a supported sample tag and price board, rather than adding another
shelf, screen, plinth and lantern. All omitted designs remain in the export.
The native review also found a fitting curtain hidden by a wardrobe; wall
placement now prefers clear spans and the curtain is visible beside the mirror.
The final whole-room imported texture estimates include shell, floor, existing
furniture, both shop kits and accents. Native scene nodes and light counts are
recorded per room/mode in native-room-budgets.json. Shared cast atlases and the
procedural core light texture are separate. The art review scene's node count
does not substitute for the live builder's future gameplay/collision-node gate.
The final maximum is 49 art-scene nodes, one core fire light, and 2,993,136
imported room-texture bytes. The 3 MB showroom margin is small: after changing
its base furnishing or shell, rerun the day/evening budget gate before adding
more dressing.

The old work order names validate_features_home.py as a shop style validator.
That script is an A1 home validator: it requires the whole home/furniture catalog
and historical geometry symmetry/counts, and has no specialist-shop manifest
interface. The current independent art_shops_b checks validate this painted kit;
the existing current art_home section is also included in regression checks.
