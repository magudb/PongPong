Equipping the playable cast

Implementation follow-up: selected tools and equipped weapons now have a fitted
held-object renderer on all eight shipped bodies and a persistent current-item
HUD. See docs/art/held-equipment/review.txt for the production art, current carry
pose scope, saved prompts and QA. The concept clothing and extended support-hand
poses described below remain separate work; this follow-up adds no wardrobe.

The player should recognize their hero first and their equipment second. Clothing
changes the silhouette and fabric; a held tool or weapon makes the current action
clear. The same item is fitted to each hero rather than stretching one overlay
across every body. Keep the approved painted storybook finish: warm pencil edges,
matte linen, honey leather, sage, cream, muted lavender and small brass details.

This delivery is an equipment design, concept art and an inspectable art studio.
It does not install a second inventory, wardrobe, combat controller or save format.
The concepts have one authored view. They are not fitted animation layers.

The eight heroes

  Rasmus / animal_fox / fox: ears above hat brim, muzzle clear, tail behind tunic.
  Kiki / animal_monkey / monkey: rounded ear openings, curled tail clear of belt.
  Kloevermynte / fairy_meadow / fairy: hair buns and purple wings remain readable.
  Birk / fairy_birk / fairy_birk: auburn hair and blue wings; removable leaf cap.
  Bertil / hobbit_pip / hobbit_pip: roomy short torso, curls and pointed ears.
  Bodil / hobbit_bodil / hobbit_bodil: braids outside collar, hat behind front hair.
  Pedro / ghost_bu / ghost_bu: round blue-white wisp, floating hands, scalloped hem.
  Hviske / ghost_hviske / ghost_hviske: the same ghost silhouette in pink/lilac.

Hero, skin and body IDs come from lua/config/characters.lua. Retired skins are
not additional production bodies. Animal, fairy, hobbit and ghost are fit families,
not permission to share pixel masks. Fox and monkey require their own fitted art;
the two fairies and two hobbits also have individual hair and silhouette masks.
Pedro/Hviske may share geometry only after an automated alpha-and-pose equality check.
Their pigment/material treatment remains separate. Do not silently share a rig
because two characters happen to have the same frame size.

Three initial looks

  Work: straw hat, cream linen, patched brown trousers or a wing-friendly skirt,
        soft leather and useful seams. A watering can or hoe makes the role clear.
  Forest: soft green cap, moss shirt/leaf tunic, dark brown lower layer, leaf clasp.
          A compact sword, staff, spear, dagger or club gives a clear outer shape.
  Festival: leaf circlet, cream/lavender embroidery and small brass jewellery.
            No large weapon silhouette in the social pose.

These are art directions for the existing clothing catalog, not three locked
costumes. Individual slots remain mixable. Clothing has no class, sex or race
restriction; the fit changes with the body. Hobbits show bare toes through an
ankle-wrap version of shoes. Ghost footwear becomes paired hem tabs/charms, and
trousers become an under-hem band. The same item ID, ownership and existing perks
apply; the presentation does not grant extra stats. Ghost shirts/dresses are short
open-sided layers around the torso, never human legs under a sheet. Wings and
tails belong to the body and cannot be covered accidentally by a universal tunic.

Slots and ownership

Keep the existing nine wardrobe slots and seven visible wardrobe cells:

  Head       hat OR circlet           Neck      necklace
  Body       shirt OR dress          Ears      earrings
  Legs       pants                    Wrist     bracelet
  Feet       shoes

Equipping a dress clears shirt and pants. Equipping shirt or pants clears dress.
Hat and circlet replace one another. A displaced garment stays owned. Resolve all
exclusions and inventory checks before committing one outfit change; publish one
outfit_changed after the whole operation. Removing clothing reveals the safe
base underlayer; it must not expose remnants of the old baked-in outfit.
Use lua/data/wardrobe_rules.lua for exclusions and slot ordering, existing
lua/items/clothing.lua IDs, and lua/data/starter_kit.lua for new-game clothing.
Do not copy these into a competing gameplay rules file.

Tools and weapons retain EconomyManager's existing tool_* and weapon slots.
The reserved accessory slot does not become a second clothing store. A selected
backpack item is not necessarily equipped, and an equipped weapon is not always
in the hand. A potion or seed packet is a temporary action presentation, not a
new saved equipment slot. No shield/offhand mechanic or backpack capacity bonus
is introduced by the art. A future cosmetic bag would need a separate decision;
the little bags in existing stock art are not functional inventory containers.

What appears in the hands

Use one presentation resolver owned by the player/action controller:
  active action item -> selected usable tool -> drawn equipped weapon -> empty.
Only one action owns the hands. Two-handed tools reserve both grips. A separate
lantern/system light does not add a persistent offhand slot. During an action,
snapshot the source item and action token; item swaps take effect after release
or explicit cancellation. A stale async asset load must not replace a newer item.
Hand visibility clears on cancel, death, sleep, dialogue and scene exit. Flying
uses a fitted hover/flight grip; the actor's altitude is applied exactly once.
The visual resolver never changes damage, tool power or inventory ownership.

Weapon silhouette guide

  Sword: broad short iron blade, little leaf-shaped guard, brown leather grip.
  Dagger: compact flint/thorn shape, visibly shorter than sword, no huge guard.
  Spear: slim timber shaft with one readable leaf/iron point; two-hand thrust.
  Staff: bent hazel with a modest leaf/crystal tip; warm local effect when used.
  Club: rounded carved branch head, clearly heavier than the sword.

Keep weapons friendly to the world: worn natural materials, restrained iron and
small botanical motifs. Rarity improves silhouette, engraving and one focal
detail; a hue swap alone is not the design. Unique/legendary effects stay within
the planned rarity vocabulary, never cover the face or create a permanent full
screen glow. The five families and planned catalog of 24 weapons belong to the
existing dungeon addendum; two swords already exist in tools.lua. This art pass
does not add damage numbers, new rarity tiers or affixes.

Tools read by head shape: hoe blade, axe wedge, pickaxe crossbar, watering spout,
fishing rod and line. Held sprites are drawn for their grip/perspective, not scaled
inventory icons. The supplied front-view object studies establish materials and
silhouettes; production needs directional held views and action grips.

Wardrobe and hands screen

Open the planned Garderobe from the existing wardrobe input. Put a large live
hero preview in the center, the seven clothing cells beside it, and an owned-item
grid with name, slot, rarity word/pips and existing perk text. A separate Hands
tab shows the equipped weapon, tools and hotbar; it does not repeat clothing.
Selecting an owned item previews it locally. Equip/Remove commits explicitly;
closing/back cancels an uncommitted preview. Show which slots a dress will replace
before applying it. Drag/drop is optional; click/tap and keyboard/controller paths
must support every action. Rotate preview, zoom, and idle/walk/action toggles help
inspect fit. Keep focus on the chosen item after an update and preserve filters.
Locked/unowned entries explain their source rather than offering an Equip button.

Work / Forest / Festival map onto the existing three planned outfit presets.
They store clothing IDs, not copies of items or a new combat loadout. Loading a
preset skips missing owned pieces and reports the omissions together. It never
duplicates, buys or destroys equipment. Race changes refit the current owned
outfit atomically. Static character-select portraits continue to show stock art;
the wardrobe preview is the equipped view. Jewellery is assessed in that close
preview and in item icons, not promised to be legible at normal world zoom.

Rendering contract

IsoChibiSprite2D remains the animation clock. A fitted layer/attachment samples
its current_clip, direction_index and rendered region after the body updates.
The current frame is int(sprite.region_rect.position.x / FRAME_SIZE); a future
read-only pose accessor could expose that without reading the private _frame.
No independent AnimationPlayer, free-running timer or second facing calculation.
Frame canvases remain 256x256; body ground anchor stays (128,200). Directions are
S, SW, W, NW, N, NE, E, SE. Store explicit pose records for every required frame
and direction. An art mirror is never an unreviewed swap of left/right hands.

The build pipeline must prepare base-body, hair/front-hair, wing, tail and
hand/forearm occluder parts before painting fitted garments. Current stock sheets
have baked outfits and hands. Drawing another shirt over them cannot remove a
long sleeve, dress hem or existing belt. Preserve the dressed stock sheets for
selection/fallback until a COMPLETE base + starter outfit is ready. Resolve a
base only when all required layers exist; never show an undressed fallback hero.

Per-pose body-space sockets: head, neck, torso, waist, foot_l, foot_r, grip_l,
grip_r and back. Each grip carries position, angle/basis, depth and visibility;
a two-handed item also stores the support grip and the authored hand pose.
Coordinates are in the same untrimmed 256x256 body canvas, not screen/world px.
Each held asset has its own grip pivot. Transform the grip pivot to the sampled
body socket, then apply actor transform/altitude once. Trimmed atlas rectangles
retain their canvas offsets so packing never moves a cuff or handle.

Painter's order is explicit per direction: rear attachment/garment parts, rear
body features, body, clothing ordered by wardrobe slot, front hands/hair/ear and
tail masks as appropriate, front attachment parts, then small local effects.
Split a held object into rear/front parts when it crosses the torso; hands must
occlude the handle. The exact front hand is pose data, not guessed from weapon
position.y. Outfit and equipment parts stay inside the actor's y-sort subtree;
do not assign global z_index values that lift a weapon over the room's furniture.
Preserve the existing WardrobeLayers sibling-halo and child-order contract.

Mirror sprite.position/rotation/scale/modulate and the offset delta relative to
base_sprite_offset. Damage flash and dodge spin affect body, clothes and weapon
together. Fairy wings keep translucent edges; ghost body material and worn cloth
material are independent. A uniform tint cannot turn all cloth into ghost skin.
Long spears and rods use trimmed attachment atlases with explicit bounds instead
of clipping them into the body cell. Trails/effects use their own small resources.

Data and loading

WardrobeManager (planned F5) owns clothing state/presets and its save section.
EconomyManager owns the already implemented tool/weapon selection. Future Arsenal
or dungeon services must integrate through those contracts, not duplicate them.
An EquipmentVisuals presenter consumes immutable outfit + held-item snapshots;
it owns no inventory, save data, combat hitbox or item rarity rule. An art registry
maps (body ID, garment design, clip group) to packed rectangles and occluders, and
(body ID, weapon family, action) to sockets. Item definitions select art IDs.
See render-contract.example.json for a proposed record shape, not authored poses.

Preload the selected locomotion groups before committing an outfit. Request
action/flight groups before the action begins and keep a complete prior outfit
while loading. Cache by body/design/palette/group; release previous groups with
reference counts. Never decode textures or rebuild atlases in the frame loop.
Do not load the whole 96-garment catalog for one character. Trim transparent
rectangles, share material/palette resources, and pack compatible parts together.
All eight directions still need authored fit checks even when color variants
share one drawing. Existing 30-layer design IDs are a starting inventory, not a
claim that painted and animated layers already exist in this checkout.

Proposed production gates (targets, not measurements of the concept art)
  <=32 MiB incremental resident locomotion textures for one fully dressed hero;
  <=16 additional CanvasItems and <=16 additional draws in the representative
  hero scene before trails; profile the actual renderer to confirm batching;
  <=0.3 ms equipment update for one hero on the reference development host;
  no missing frame/direction/occluder records and no synchronous loads mid-action.
Use padded compressed-block + mip accounting, and separately report shared body
and portrait atlases. Re-measure on target hardware; don't reuse the old prototype
timings from PLAN_2D_FEATURES as measurements of these painted assets.

Implementation sequence

Progress: the first two-body fit proof now lives in docs/art/equipment-fit/.
It supplies Rasmus/Pedro layered idle/walk/run/reach poses, grips, hand/ear
occlusion and a native review gallery. Motion is an offline puppet bake; it is
not the full production clip/wardrobe integration gate. The original flattened
concepts and illustrative schema below remain unchanged historical design work.

  1. Fit proof: Rasmus + Pedro, starter shirt/pants/shoes, hat and sword. Author all
     idle/walk/run directions plus one action; test hand masks, turns and spin.
  2. Complete their required clips before enabling those wardrobe bases. Keep
     base + starter layer availability atomic in CharacterCatalog resolution.
  3. Fit the remaining six heroes; special checks for Birk/fairy wing roots,
     Bodil's braids, monkey tail and the two ghost material treatments.
  4. Wire the existing wardrobe ownership/preset/perk contracts and screen.
     Add held-item presentation beside the existing action controller, with no
     changes to combat timing or item balance just to match an illustration.
  5. Expand garment silhouettes and five weapon-family poses; bake directional
     silhouettes, sockets and occlusion from one reviewed pose source.

Acceptance matrix

Every live hero x every supported garment slot x all eight directions x all
required idle/walk/run/action/flight frames. Also: hat/circlet and dress exclusions;
mixed outfits; equipped weapon vs active tool; one/two-hand transitions; rapid
selection during loads; flight/landing; damage flash; dodge spin; room y-sort;
missing art; race switch; cancel/death/dialogue; saved preset missing an item.
Check gameplay ownership/stats independently from pixels. Review at normal world
zoom and wardrobe close-up on both light and dark floors, with automated native
captures for known poses. Visual continuity must be reviewed across adjacent
frames; a valid JSON rig is not proof of clothing fit.

The supplied art studio is for reviewing these choices. Its outfit images are
flattened concept drawings, and its equipment objects are front-view studies.
It does not pretend they are production-ready animated garment layers.
