Wardrobe and loadout implementation

Open Wardrobe with G or Pause > Wardrobe while the character is free. Clothes
and Hands are separate pages of the existing book theme. The game pauses while
the screen is open. Changing clothes is available anywhere; the user did not
choose the optional home-only restriction.

Select pieces to try them together locally, across clothing categories and the
Hands page. Equip commits only the selected piece and its explicit exclusions;
other try-ons remain local until individually equipped. Remove
returns a worn item to owned stock. Closing discards uncommitted try-ons. Browse
all previews the 96 authored clothing IDs without granting them. New games own
the existing cream dress and wear the existing cream shirt, brown trousers and
boots. Clothing still comes through the existing shops and loot systems.

Seven clothing cells cover hat/circlet, necklace, earrings, body, trousers,
bracelet and shoes. Dresses replace shirt and trousers. Hats and circlets exclude
one another. Work, Forest and Festival save clothing snapshots; wearing a preset
reports missing pieces together and never creates replacement items.

Hands selects owned weapons and tools, changes real equipment and assigns or
clears the existing eight-slot hotbar. Its character preview uses the same
HeldEquipment2D renderer as the world. Selection is a preview until Equip or a
hotbar assignment. The actual current-item HUD remains the gameplay readout.
Keyboard, mouse and controller use existing navigation and focus conventions.

Ownership and persistence

WardrobeManager owns clothing stock, equipped slots and the three outfit presets.
EconomyManager routes clothing to that store; it remains the sole owner of tools
and weapons. The player retains the existing hotbar. Worn clothing cannot be
sold. Lua retains item stats, set bonuses, slot exclusions and drawing order.
Perks combines the worn points with existing caps. Completed collections grant
capstones through LootService. SaveManager registers wardrobe at order 30;
legacy saves adopt backpack clothing before filling missing starter pieces.
No second inventory, equipment save section or animation clock is introduced.

Art and rendering

Built-in image generation produced saved painted masters from the shipped body
references. Exact prompts and selected source paths are in prompts.json. All
selected sources are copied into tools/art/drawn/sources/wardrobe/. Four new safe
bases retain the humanoids' identities, wings, hair and braids; original stock
animations and portraits remain byte-identical. Fur and wisp bodies use stock
art. Ghost clothes use wraps, hem bands and charms. Hobbit toe occlusion keeps
bare feet visible. Neck pendants are hidden in rear views.

Run tools/art/.venv/bin/python tools/art/drawn/wardrobe.py to pack the sources.
assets/wardrobe/manifest.json records source hashes and per-body, per-direction
fit rectangles. It includes all 30 garment families on all eight bodies, four
safe bases and eight functional occlusion masks (252 production rasters).
The repaired ghost lower set has seven main rows plus an explicit eighth SE
source; no missing view is silently mirrored.

WardrobeLayers is shared by world and preview. Its static eight-direction RGB
paintings use the existing body's exact inverse puppet motion field in a shared
shader include. The real clip, frame and direction drive every layer. Selected
textures alone remain referenced; no action loads a clothing atlas. S3TC with
mips bounds each 256x2048 atlas to about 0.67 MiB. Palette mapping retains the
source pigment, shadows, highlights and warm ink. These are RGB paintings, not
legacy lossless palette-index textures.

Every drawing root inherits the actual source Sprite's final scale, rotation,
altitude and tint once. The matching body mask restores face, ears, hands, wings,
braids and toes only where clothing overlaps. Complete selected resources are
prepared before changing the visible outfit. Hero changes refit the same actor;
world travel can reparent it without destroying its presentation. Held equipment
uses the same body material for its foreground hand patch. The four new safe
bases have reviewed hand centres, tracked offline through the same puppet field
into explicit per-frame records; clothing masks and bracelets use those centres
too. Hand masks retain painted skin and its edge, excluding vest/shorts pixels.
This avoids restoring a square of base clothing or skin through a long dress.

Validation and limits

See docs/qa/loadout/ for reproducible reports and actual Godot captures. Review
covers all 96 palettes on Rasmus, all 30 families on the seven other bodies,
all directions in mixed outfits, real save/load, local cancellation, pad equip,
hotbar assignment, legacy adoption and existing weapon/tool behavior. Frame-lock
checks exercise all authored clip/frame/direction combinations. These are not a
claim that every stock animation frame received a separate pixel review.
Existing art retains its prior visual dispositions when byte-identical.

Anatomical fitting follow-up (2026-10-17)

The earlier rectangular fits remain the source UV coordinates, not final body
positions. assets/wardrobe/tailoring.json now records the actual torso/head
centres, neck, waist, ankle, shoulder/hip/foot widths and cuff drop for each hero.
In particular, a tail offsets the fox torso from the 128-pixel ground pivot.
The fairies and hobbits use the proportions of their safe-base paintings, whose
waists differ from those in the older stock references. Birk has his own wider
foot fit. The ghost adaptations retain wraps/bands/charms and a wisp silhouette.

WardrobeTailoring maps each design onto those landmarks in all eight directions.
The inverse fit shapes the shoulder/waist/hem and lowers outer sleeves instead
of flattening the whole garment into one rectangle. The stock motion field then
moves the fitted cloth with the body, using the same frame and direction. The
skin occluder samples exactly the same fitted garment coverage. Face masks follow
the actual head position; front paws/skin cover cloth without a round patch of
fur or underclothes. Rebuild only these technical masks with:
  tools/art/.venv/bin/python tools/art/drawn/wardrobe.py --masks-only

Restrained side shading gives fabric curvature. Dye weights remain normalized
for unusual painted pixels, and a dark reference pigment cannot wash a pale
replacement dye's folds to white. Source paintings/garment atlases are unchanged.
No extra animation clock, generated pose textures or drawing nodes are added.
Only the changed slot receives a new sprite/material; unchanged clothing keeps
its own texture, pigment and node. A body change still refits the entire outfit.

Try-ons retain per-slot choices when browsing another category. The screen says
that Equip changes only this piece, lists shirt/trouser or hat/circlet exclusions,
and labels pending previews. Removing an item shows the result immediately.
Closing discards pending previews. Loading a saved outfit intentionally replaces
the whole outfit and clears previews. Ownership, perks and save schema are unchanged.

Native evidence, animation sheets, per-slot regression checks and limits are in
docs/qa/clothing-fit/. Future painting-repair prompts are saved in
fitting-prompts.json; this fitting pass did not generate or repaint garment art.
