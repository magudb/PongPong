Rasmus and Pedro fitted equipment proof — 15 October 2026

Continues the next art step on codex-review after the festival kit. This package
is isolated presentation/art tooling. No m1b gameplay is merged, no live hero
fallback is switched, and no inventory, wardrobe ownership, combat, save or
CharacterCatalog rules change.

Delivered

Two safe base turnarounds, four separately removable garments per body, one
shared iron sword with eight authored views, body-space hand/ear occluders,
and explicit grips for all eight directions of idle, walk, run and reach.
There are 160 poses per body, 320 total and 640 explicit grip records. Direction
order is S, SW, W, NW, N, NE, E, SE; no source direction is mirrored at export.
The four clips match the existing IsoChibiSprite2D frame counts/rates.

The selected five imagegen masters are in tools/art/drawn/sources/equipment_fit/:
rasmus_base.png, pedro_base.png, rasmus_garments_v2.png, pedro_garments.png and
sword.png. rasmus_garments.png is the rejected pre-repair input retained because
the saved correction prompt references it. It is not exported. Exact generation
and targeted correction prompts, input roles and tool mode are in prompts.json.
All images used the built-in imagegen tool, not the CLI/API fallback.

The chosen matte gouache/watercolor/pencil style remains authoritative. Rasmus's
face, ears, cream fur and tail stay visible. Pedro has a rounded floating wisp,
short opaque cream shirt, brown woven hem band and paired leather hem charms;
he never gains human legs or boots. The same existing clothing item IDs map to
those body-specific presentations. Garment removal reveals each safe base,
rather than remnants of an old baked costume. The hat repair removes baked fox
ears and corrects a front-facing shirt in the rear-right row.

The frames are an offline continuous 2D puppet bake from the separate paintings,
using the established painted pack's motion vocabulary. They are not individually
hand-painted walk/action poses. Every body/cloth/ear/hand part shares exactly one
sampling field. Grip positions and local bases are solved against that field
(maximum recorded inverse error below 0.0001 px). The sword remains rigid and
has an authored per-direction angle plus a short reach accent. This is a reach
presentation proof, not a finished attack animation or combat timing change.
Far-side sword/hand regions can be occluded by the body and Rasmus's large tail;
a future attack needs dedicated extended arm poses, not a global front z plane.

Export and native review

Rebuild:
  tools/art/.venv/bin/python tools/art/drawn/pack.py --only=equipment-fit

Review:
  tools/dev/play.sh --throwaway tools/art/drawn/equipment_fit_gallery.tscn

Arrows turn the close previews; H changes hero; 1–4 select idle/walk/run/reach;
F flashes, S spins, and the five independent toggles remove individual garments
or the sword. Buttons expose the same controls. The eight small figures show
all directions; close views compare painted timber with shaded forest tiles.
There is no Equip action and no new gameplay screen.

All 320 baked poses appear in the eight native frame contact sheets. Additional
captures show both floor palettes, hat/shirt removal, and shared spin/altitude/
tint. Files are in native/. The source drawings and native imports were inspected
for identity, rear views, cloth fit, hem/ear/tail clearance and adjacent-frame
continuity. Native source size is 256x256 with ground anchor (128,200); atlas trims
retain that canvas offset. File names and source hashes are in packing.json.

The presenter lives entirely under tools/art/drawn/. It samples the stock actor's
current_clip, direction_index and rendered region AFTER the actor ticks, with
no second animation timer and no private frame access. The fitted group mirrors
Sprite position, rotation, scale, tint and spin offset delta exactly once. All
pieces stay in the actor subtree with no elevated global z index. An unsupported
clip switches the entire proof off and shows the complete dressed stock hero;
there is no incomplete production base. Proof textures load during studio setup,
never in sync_pose. Leaving the studio releases its selected resources.

Budgets and limits

Atlases are lossless RGBA8 WebP imports, no mipmaps, with two clear gutter pixels
around every packed rectangle. These are final painted colors, not tint-ready
clothing data. Do not put them in the tint-driven wardrobe layer directory or
apply a uniform tint as a garment recoloring system. Small-scale close review
uses linear filtering; target-device minification still needs later validation.

Locomotion residency counts complete idle/walk/run proof atlases, including body,
all starter clothing, hands/ears and sword. Rasmus is 27.56 MiB and Pedro 18.36 MiB,
each below the proposed 32 MiB target. Reach adds approximately 6.94 MiB / 4.58 MiB.
The studio deliberately holds those four groups at once. Shared stock sheets
used by the animation clock, tile textures, scene overhead and GPU pools are
separate. The integrated presenter should stop needing duplicate stock body
sheets when a complete production body exists. This proof is not evidence that
the full wardrobe catalog fits these budgets.

Each figure adds one group and nine Sprite2D nodes: ten CanvasItems, below the
16-item target. That is a node bound, not a measured renderer draw-call claim.
No light, body collider, navigation obstacle or combat hitbox is added. CPU update
sampling on this development host is saved separately in cpu-profile.json; it
excludes setup, renderer/GPU time and target-device frame time. Lossless texture
size/residency and build time are recorded in packing.json. Production still
needs hardware draw-call, mip/filtering, streaming and total actor memory QA.

To update the CPU sample explicitly (ordinary tests leave the saved sample alone):
