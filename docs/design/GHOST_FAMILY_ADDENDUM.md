The seven cases of the decision are a table in `economy_check6.py` (owned / registry / room -> payload, bit, line). How common is the fallback? A player who opened an `fe` chest with the wanderer or killed the snurresvamp for its pool before 48 / 80 finds (a typical player reaches them after about 12 and 20 game weeks): plausible, so it is a named behaviour, not an error path. **The alternatives are the user's choice, `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` Q5:** non-keepsake items for steps 4 and 5 (the ladder then has fewer than five rarities), NEW family-only keepsakes (the family as their one fixed source; they need item rows, icons and an art bake of their own), or removing the two lamps from the dungeon tables (a change of the dungeon track, not of the family).
* **Hearts.** 5 points a find (a twentieth of a heart), two finds a day at most, six hide days a week: **12 finds = 60 points = 0.6 heart a week** at the very most; the SV rule "reward points <= one heart per event" holds (5 <= 100); the weekly chat stays the heart engine (100 points).
* **Pace** (a game day is 10 real minutes, a week 70 real minutes; a stated profile, not a measurement): a **casual** player (1.5 finds a week) reaches the five steps after 2.0 / 6.7 / 16 / 32 / 53 game weeks; a **typical** one (4 a week) after 0.8 / 2.5 / 6 / 12 / 20 weeks = **0.9 / 2.9 / 7.0 / 14 / 23 real hours**; a **dedicated** one (every slot, 12 a week) after 0.2 / 0.8 / 2 / 4 / 6.7 weeks (0.3 / 1.0 / 2.3 / 4.7 / 7.8 hours). A game year is 16 weeks, so the legendary is a two-year chase for a typical player on purpose.
* **Per-day cap and no failure state.** Two rewarded finds a day (one per slot); nothing is lost by missing a slot; the counter never decreases.
