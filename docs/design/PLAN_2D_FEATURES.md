**The three new user requirements (verbatim, 2026-10-12):**
1. "the player needs to be able to upgrade their home from small shed to grand castle (10 step)"
2. "we need to be able to build, buy and find furniture ranking from normal, uncommon, rare, unique, legendary"
3. "the player needs to be able to find clothing, shoes, pants, dresses, shirts, hats, jewellery (normal, uncommon, rare, unique, legendary)"

How to use this file: sections 1-3 say WHAT is decided and which contracts change; section 4 says WHO does it and in which wave; sections 5-6 say HOW assets are produced and how the result is proven; section 7 is the honest list of what is weak, stubbed or cut. Where this addendum and `PLAN.reviewed.md` disagree about a feature topic, **this addendum wins**; for everything else the plan stays binding (its section R amendments, contracts and decision register D1-D31 are untouched).

### 2.4 Clothing and the wardrobe

**Art approach (decided on spike evidence): tint-ready layers over naked base heroes** (approaches A+B of the wardrobe spike).
- Every garment is a skinned shell on the SAME rig as the body, baked per garment as ONE trimmed, packed, **lossless** WebP atlas per body bake and clip group. The body is rendered only as an unlit depth OCCLUDER, so every layer composites ABOVE the body sprite in all 8 directions (tail through a dress, far sleeve, ears through a hat are already in the pixels).
- Texel = `R = shade x wA, G = shade x wB, B = shade x wC, A = coverage` (three tint regions, shade is the sRGB-encoded lit grey); the shader multiplies by the item palette (`art.palette` = 3 hex colours) -> colourway, dye and rarity cost **zero extra sheets** (a colour-baked 5 x 4 family would be 20.6 MB against 0.72 MB). The shader equals the Python mirror within 0.06/255 mean (max 1).
- **Formats are a hard rule:** lossy WebP and S3TC destroy the packed channels (S3TC mean error 14-19/255, blotchy; lossy q97 PSNR about 30 dB and bigger than lossless); BPTC would be fine but does not exist in GL Compatibility on macOS. Layers = lossless WebP `exact=True`, RGBA8 in VRAM; base colour sheets stay S3TC.
- **Scopes** (garments are baked per scope, not per hero): body (shirt, pants, dress, necklace, bracelet): T = fox_base (shared by fox, rabbit, hedgehog, hobbit: alpha mismatch 0.08-0.20, visually identical), S = squirrel_base (native: the big tail leaves a notch from behind when borrowed), F = fairy_base; head (hat, circlet, earrings): fox (squirrel borrows with dy +2), rabbit, hedgehog, hobbit, fairy native (the borrowed fits mismatch 0.28-1.1); feet (shoes): stout (fox, rabbit, hedgehog, squirrel), hobbit, fairy.
- **Bodies:** ONE authoring scale 0.80 for the T family so torso/arms/legs are pixel-identical across species (rabbit ears +16 %, hedgehog -7 % against the pack sprites: rebake heroes, never mix with the old `fox`/`rabbit`/`hedgehog` sheets); fairy keeps its scale. NPCs and farm animals keep baked-in outfits. Skins: fox, rabbit, hedgehog, squirrel -> `*_base`; hobbit Pip / Moss / Bramble -> `hobbit_*_base`; fairy meadow / stardust / dusk -> `fairy_base`, `fairy_stardust_base`, `fairy_dusk_base`. The four recolour skins reuse the parent's layers only if their occluder alpha is pixel-identical (checked by A2).
- **Honest quality:** same family as the cast, 7.5/10: shading, outline and proportions match; no folds, stitching or brushwork; hobbit hats are huge (they enclose the curls), fairy hats small on the big head, fairy shirts barely show, fox boots small and dark; **jewellery is a 2-4 px detail at zoom 0.4-0.6** (readable in the Garderobe live preview, the item icons and 1:1 close-ups - NOT in portraits: `portraits/<base>.webp` is one bust with the starter outfit, RV-A8); **rarity of clothes is readable in the world only for unique and legendary (aura), see 2.1**; in Phase A each body slot has only 2 silhouettes and 3 hat designs serve all 18 hats, and most unique/legendary garments share a normal garment's shell until Phase B; shirt and pants icons are hollow shells (pants need a long-leg icon mode). Strongest: dresses, the cap / straw / wizard hats on fox and hobbit, dungarees, the equipped busts.

**Runtime (F5):** `WardrobeLayers` (child of the hero `IsoChibiSprite2D`, `process_priority 100`) polls clip, direction and frame of the body and sets `region_rect`/`position` of 1-4 `Sprite2D` parts per layer from the packed rect table (frame index = direction x frames + frame); layers are ordered by `slot z (+ per-design z, e.g. dungarees 22, + optional per-direction z_dir)` as CHILD ORDER, never `z_index`; legendary halos are sibling sprites BEFORE the body sprite. Lock-step proven by 8,960 checks (fox, hobbit, fairy x all clips x 8 directions), fairy flight and altitude included. Added by this addendum: **the layers container and the halo container mirror the body sprite's position, rotation, scale, modulate and texture-offset shift every frame AND both shaders multiply the inherited modulate (`varying vec4 vmod`)** (V6: without the mirror clothes stay upright and un-flashed during the dodge roll and damage flash; without the shader change a mirrored modulate is silently dropped - RV-A1). Loading: `loco` group (idle/walk/run) synchronously on equip (3-22 ms per layer), `act` and `flight` groups on worker threads, `unload_group` when entering the forest. Cost: +7 draw calls per fully dressed hero, 100 dressed heroes 8.6 ms/frame (bare 3.3 ms); one 7-layer outfit 49 MB all clips resident or about 17 MB loco only.

**Slots and rules** (`lua/data/wardrobe_rules.lua`): nine equip slots pants z10, shoes 12, shirt 20, dress 21, bracelet 25, necklace 30, earrings 35, hat 40, circlet 41, shown as **seven Garderobe cells** Hoved (hat | circlet), Hals, Ører, Krop (shirt | dress), Ben, Håndled, Fødder; exclusive pairs {dress, shirt}, {dress, pants}, {hat, circlet} (equipping empties the other slot, the piece stays owned). Worn pieces cannot be sold. Starter outfit (2.7).

**Perks formula (binding, content):** `gear(kind) = sum of worn item perks + the best 2 wardrobe_sets bonuses` (kinds speed, energy, luck, discount); `gear_capped = min(gear, perk_caps[kind])` (speed 0.15, energy 20, luck 0.20, discount 0.15). `furniture(kind)` = all placed furniture-set bonuses + hygge level (energy, luck). `home(energy) = tier max_energy_bonus`. `race(discount)` = hobbit 0.10. Then `speed = gear_capped`, `energy = min(gear_capped + furniture + home, energy_total 60)`, `luck = min(gear_capped + furniture, 0.20)`; **one discount owner (RV-P5):** `Perks.total("discount") = gear_capped + furniture + race` (no friend term) and `PriceCurve.final_price` multiplies the price by `(1 - min(Perks.total("discount") + friend discount, discount_total 0.20))`; there is no second hobbit factor in `get_buy_price`. Numeric contract: a 1600-gold rare piece costs a fox with gear 0.10 and 10 friend hearts (0.05) 1360, the same hobbit 1280, a hobbit with neither 1440. Best piece in every slot reaches speed 15 %, luck 19 %, energy 28 (clamped 20), discount 10 %. Consumers: Player2D speed and stamina max (P06), LootService luck (F4), ShopSystem discount (P12). Garderobe shows every line with "maks." at the cap (`Perks.breakdown`).

## 5. Asset production plan

All art comes from the same procedural pipeline as the cast (Python/VTK generator, one orthographic 36-degree camera, three lights, warm outline `(75,60,73)`, 87.074 px/m, sprites never scaled). Two new art packages produce everything; the long jobs run on the lead's machine in the background.

### 5.1 Preconditions (all from the plan or measured by the spikes)

- `tools/art/setup_env.sh` (P08, about 2 min) gives `tools/art/.venv` (Python 3.12, numpy scipy Pillow vtk); `export PY=$PWD/tools/art/.venv/bin/python`. P08 carries the 4-line generator patch (`export(..., rig=None, anim_from=None)`, `m.scale_fixed`).
- `tools/import/write_import_stubs.py` (P05) before the first `godot --import`; no Godot editor open on the repo; `tools/art/.gdignore` hides `tools/art/` and its `_stage/` from the importer.
- Quiet machine: another heavy bake running at the same time made the spike's first timings 2-6x too slow. Long jobs: `run_in_background` (a trailing `&` inside one tool call is killed when the call ends). zsh does not word-split unquoted variables: all loops are bash scripts.
- Bake hygiene learnt in the spikes: reset the camera every frame, premultiplied downsampling, Python module names ASCII only, `m.base_height` keeps px/m constant, VTK 9.6 `cells.SetCells` is deprecated (use `ImportLegacyFormat`).

### 5.5 Failure modes already known (so nobody rediscovers them)

Icons are NOT downsampled world sprites (they would keep the warm world outline and the ground shadow and fail the ink-ring test); a layer or halo shader that does not multiply `vmod` silently drops every flash, fade and night grade; the dark night tile eats ink outlines (1.25:1) - legendary icons need the pale rim. Hats must sit above the eye line (derived `hat_v`) or the brim covers the face from the 36-degree camera; ring bands must be offset >= shell offset + tube radius; hair-under-hat is solved only by oversizing the hat to the head + hair envelope (hobbit hats are huge); the fairy bun is stripped from `fairy_base` so hats fit (it can return as an accessory layer later); body must be rendered unlit black, no MSAA, SS=4, premultiplied downsample for the occluder pass; pants/dungarees need `z = 22` for the bib; one authoring scale per garment family or shared layers do not fit; never mix new `*_base` sheets with the old pack `fox`/`rabbit`/`hedgehog` sheets in one outfit.

### 5.6 Fallback ladder (apply in this order if disk, VRAM, bake time or quality fails)

1. Ship phase A only (existing 15 designs): -41 MB, the 29 items with requested designs keep their fallback design and their palette.
2. Load only the `loco` group of worn layers in Hyggebyen (about 17 MB per outfit instead of 49).
3. Drop the four recolour bases (-9 MB): moss/bramble/stardust/dusk skins use the parent's base colours (skin names stay).
4. Let squirrel borrow the T body bake (-4 MB): a notch is visible from behind.
5. Share heads: rabbit and hedgehog borrow the fox head with offsets (-about 20 MB, mismatch 0.28-0.9, visibly off at the ears/spikes).
6. Ship necklace / bracelet / earrings only in Garderobe, portraits and dialogue (approach D): saves little disk (2-3 MB) but removes 4 layers per fully dressed hero.
7. Pre-composited outfits (approach C) only for fixed NPC set pieces (3.2 MB per outfit per hero).
BPTC would cut layer VRAM to a quarter but is not available in GL Compatibility on macOS; S3TC is never an option for layers.

## Appendix B. Godot / generator pitfalls for these packages (in addition to plan Appendix B)

A canvas_item shader that writes `COLOR = ...` in fragment() DISCARDS the inherited modulate/alpha: capture it in `vertex()` (`varying vec4 vmod = COLOR`) and multiply; reading `COLOR` in fragment() gives the texel, not the modulate; `Script.callv(&"static_fn", args)` calls a static function of a `load()`ed GDScript without a class reference (late binding); additive blending of a glow is invisible on bright ground, use normal alpha; a validator that reads files finished by later waves must assemble a stage tree, not skip.

New `class_name` scripts need `--import` before first use; `-s` drivers compile before autoloads exist (use `Svc.node`); JSON numbers are floats (`int()` before any dictionary key or index, `str(rot)` gives "0.0"); `Vector2i` stringifies to the STRING "(3, 4)" in JSON: save cells as `[x, z]`; `TileMapLayer.map_to_local` is the tile CENTRE (subtract half a tile in every cell formula); never use `z_index` for layer order (global, breaks y-sorting): child order only; the halo sprite must be a sibling inserted before the body sprite; lossless WebP with `exact=True` (RGB under alpha 0 is data); never run layer data through a colour-managed path; a hand-written `.import` stub is honoured on first import (floors: no mipmaps; bases: S3TC; layers: none); an empty Lua table `{}` is an Array; fairy skirts are rigid body-group (legs swing through the hem: fine for chibi); wide sprites sort by their left-front corner and nothing may stand behind them; `rm -f dir/*` is blocked by the safety check (write to fresh directories); a heavy bake on the same machine distorts timings 2-6x.
